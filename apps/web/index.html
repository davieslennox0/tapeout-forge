From netlist to on-chain circuit in three steps

01

Optimize

Exact synthesis up to three inputs, plus ABC and rewriting for larger functions. Pick area, depth, or balanced, and aim at a reference cost.

02

Prove

Every result is checked against the original, exhaustively up to 20 inputs and by SAT beyond that. A second, separate tool re-checks the published circuits.

03

Tape out

Copy the netlist or the ready-made tape-out calldata, send it from your own wallet, and list the circuit in the registry once it is deployed.

1 circuit exported

Forgeout exports a verified circuit as a Tiny Tapeout project: structural Verilog, the template wrapper, and the GitHub Actions flow that produces the layout. A majority-of-three test circuit has already gone through that open-source flow (davieslennox0/maj3-silicon-spike).

Ordering limitation

Tapeout-ready export. We have not ordered fabrication. Ordering is a separate paid step that the circuit owner completes on Tiny Tapeout. We do not claim a physical chip exists.

Supported: combinational circuits up to about 500 cells, with references flattened first. Latches only with a documented reset convention. Anything else is refused with a reason.

What Forgeout does not claim

The numbers above are what was measured. These are the edges of them.

No reward predictions

FabX does not publish all of its reward variables, so Forgeout reports cost C and score q only, never a payout.

46 of 100 tasks replayed

The other 54, including every sequential task, ship only sampled test vectors. Fitting those would be overfitting, so they are left out.

The reference still wins 13

Exact synthesis is proven only up to three inputs. Above that Forgeout is heuristic and sometimes loses.

No fabrication claim

We have not ordered fabrication; that is a separate paid step the circuit owner takes. On this page, tape-out means the on-chain TapeOut circuit unless it says silicon.
